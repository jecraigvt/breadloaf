A clarified New York sour keeps the warmth of bourbon and the brightness of lemon, softens their edges with milk clarification, and finishes with a red wine float.

A little chemistry, a little patience, and a clearer result: a tribute to everyone interested in the other kind of smooth proofs.

THE BASE RATIO

2 parts bourbon · 1 part lemon · 1 part syrup, clarified with 1 part milk. Make and clarify this bourbon sour base first. Add the red wine float only when serving.

Measure in mL for this batch; the original ounce measures are approximate. Start with 4,375 mL before filtering and expect about 3,500 mL after liquid is lost with the curds. Four 1-litre bottles are enough.

Making the syrup with optional salt

With a kitchen scale: combine 550 g white sugar (19.4 oz by weight), 550 g water (about 550 mL / 18.6 US fl oz), and 0.7 g fine table salt if using it. This is 1:1 sugar to water by weight. The salt is optional, but preferred.

Without a scale: use about 2¾ level US cups granulated sugar + 2⅓ US cups water, with a scant ⅛ teaspoon of fine table salt if using it. The cup measurements are approximate; they still give you enough syrup for the batch. All cups, tablespoons, and fluid ounces in this guide use US measures.

Gently heat and stir until the sugar and salt dissolve together. Avoid boiling it down. Cool completely, then measure 875 mL (about 29½ US fl oz / 3½ US cups + 3 tablespoons) for the cocktail. Save the small amount left over.

Why 550 g of each? The dissolved sugar and water volumes do not simply add together. At room temperature, 550 g sugar + 550 g water makes about 895 mL / 30.3 US fl oz of syrup, leaving about 20 mL extra. Using 525 g of each would make only about 854 mL. These estimates use the published density of 50% sucrose solution at 20°C / 68°F.

Salt amount: 0.7 g is for the full syrup preparation, including the small amount left over. It is about a scant ⅛ teaspoon of fine table salt. Salt grains vary; use the weight if your scale reads tenths of a gram.

Will clarification remove the salt? Dissolved salt passes through with the liquid. Some is lost in the liquid held by discarded curds, but it is not filtered out like suspended particles. Adding it to the syrup before clarification is fine.

03

ONCE EVERYTHING IS READY

Make the batch.

Allow 10 minutes for curdling, then a few hours for filtering. Both steps can be done at room temperature. For easier timing, make the cocktail the day before serving, then refrigerate the finished bottles.

  1. 1

    PREPARE

    Measure everything.

    Clean the containers, colander, jug, funnel, and bottles. Juice the lemons and remove seeds and as much pulp as possible. Make the syrup by gently heating 550 g sugar + 550 g water (about 2¾ US cups sugar + 2⅓ US cups water), with 0.7 g / a scant ⅛ teaspoon fine table salt (optional, but preferred). Stir until the sugar and salt dissolve together, then cool completely and measure 875 mL / about 29½ US fl oz. Label the two Cambros A and B.

  2. 2

    CAMBRO A

    Mix the bourbon sour base.

    Combine 1,750 mL / 59.2 US fl oz bourbon, 875 mL / about 29½ US fl oz lemon juice, and 875 mL / about 29½ US fl oz simple syrup in Cambro A. Stir thoroughly. Dry shaking is unnecessary for a batch this size. The optional salt is already dissolved in the syrup if using it. Do not add the wine.

  3. 3

    CAMBRO B

    Pour the cocktail into the milk.

    Put 875 mL / about 29½ US fl oz whole milk in Cambro B. Slowly pour the sour mix from A into the milk while gently stirring. The lemon acidity will make it curdle.

    The order matters: cocktail into milk. The curds will become the filter bed that clarifies the drink.
  4. 4

    10 MINUTES

    Let the curds form.

    Cover Cambro B and let it rest for about 10 minutes at room temperature. Look for visible curds and separation, then start filtering. Rinse Cambro A so it is ready to receive the filtered cocktail.

  5. 5

    THE FILTER SETUP

    Unfold. Wet. Line. Overlap.

    Set the colander securely over the clean Cambro A. Unfold the square pre-folded filters fully, then wet them. Line the colander with the opened filters, overlapping the edges a little. Cover the interior so there are no gaps for unfiltered liquid to run through.

  6. 6

    SLOW FILTRATION

    Let the curds do the work.

    Gently ladle some of the curdled mixture into the lined colander, including the curds. Catch the first cloudy liquid in the measuring jug. Once a curd bed has formed, gently pour that cloudy liquid back through the same bed.

    When the liquid runs clear, collect it in Cambro A. Add more mixture slowly as the level falls. Keep the curd bed in place, and keep the paper edges covered and overlapping. Filter at room temperature; this can take several hours.

    Do not press or squeeze the curds. Let gravity work. If the result is still cloudy, pass it through the established curd bed again.
  7. 7

    BOTTLE & CHILL

    Save the clear batch.

    Use one of the bottle kit’s included funnels to transfer the clarified cocktail into clean bottles. Close the swing-top stoppers for storage. Distribute the approximately 3.5 L finished batch between four 1-litre bottles. Label them with the date, bourbon choice, and whether you used salt. Refrigerate until serving; clarification does not remove the need for cold storage.

    Expect about 3.5 L after filtering. Some liquid stays with the curds, so the exact finished amount varies slightly.

    Some final settling after refrigeration is completely normal. A layer of sediment may collect at the bottom of the storage bottles. If desired, gently pour the clear liquid into other clean storage containers, leaving the sediment behind. This extra transfer is optional.

WHAT YOU’RE LOOKING FOR

A clear bourbon sour base.

Clarified means transparent, not necessarily colorless. A pale amber tint from the bourbon is normal. Visible haze means it needs another gentle pass through the curd bed.

Contains dairy. Milk clarification does not make this safe for a milk allergy.

04

THE FINAL STEP

For serving.

This is where it becomes a New York sour: a red wine float on top of the clarified bourbon sour.

Have these ready

Dry red wine: one 750 mL / 25.4 US fl oz bottle gives you about 50 floats at 15 mL / ½ US fl oz each.

How to choose: look for a dry red with berry or cherry flavors, moderate tannins, and modest oak. Malbec is an easy starting point. Zinfandel brings ripe fruit and spice; Cabernet Sauvignon gives a firmer, darker-fruited float. An affordable bottle you enjoy drinking is plenty; avoid very sweet reds or heavily oaked bottles that overpower the lemon and bourbon.

Familiar examples: Alamos Malbec for blackberry and dark cherry; Cline Old Vine Zinfandel for cherry, strawberry, and gentle tannins; or H3 Cabernet Sauvignon for blackberry and currant. These are commonly stocked labels, though local availability varies.

Large clear ice cubes: one per drink, made ahead.

Glasses or sturdy disposable cups: wide enough for a 2-inch / 5 cm cube, plus the sour and wine. A small spoon helps float the wine.

  1. 1

    Ice, then the clarified sour.

    Put one large clear cube in a rocks glass or cup. Pour 90–120 mL / 3–4 US fl oz of the chilled clarified bourbon sour over it.

  2. 2

    Float the red wine on top.

    Hold a spoon just above the surface, with its back facing up. Slowly pour 15 mL / ½ oz dry red wine over the back of the spoon so it settles as a layer on top. Adjust the wine amount to taste.

  3. 3

    Serve with the float intact.

    Serve immediately. Leave the wine layer on top for the New York sour finish.
